Sorting these by what actually determines their usefulness to you: how many measurements exist, and how much dynamical information each one carries. Those trade off against each other almost perfectly, which is why stratification is the right frame.

High-throughput, low-information-per-datapoint

These are your representation-learning substrate. Individually thin, collectively enormous, and all inherently comparative — which fits the contrastive framing from before.

  • Deep mutational scans. 10⁴–10⁶ variants per experiment, thousands of experiments. The readouts vary in what they encode: stability (proteolysis-based, like the ~10⁶-variant mega-scale folding datasets), binding, activity, expression, localization. ProteinGym aggregates ~2.5M mutants. Crucially, some scans are run at multiple temperatures or ligand concentrations, which turns them into response measurements.
  • Thermal proteome profiling. Melting temperature shifts across thousands of proteins simultaneously, in cells, under drug treatment or condition change. This is ∂stability/∂(ligand) at proteome scale.
  • Limited proteolysis MS. Structural change readout on whole proteomes, sensitive to conformational shifts rather than just abundance.
  • Chemical genetics and growth fitness under stress panels. pH, osmolyte, temperature, oxidant. Very noisy, very abundant, and directly encodes environmental coupling.
  • Directed evolution trajectories. Underused. Each campaign is a record of which mutations improved which property from which starting point — a partially ordered set of causal interventions.

Mid-throughput, ensemble-resolved

This is the sweet spot for your purposes: dozens to thousands of measurements, each constraining a distribution rather than a point.

  • HDX-MS. Per-peptide protection factors, which are local free energies of opening. Directly reports on conformational excursions and their thermodynamics. Thousands of published datasets, differential HDX being the most useful form.
  • NMR relaxation. Order parameters give ps–ns amplitudes of local motion; relaxation dispersion gives populations, rates, and chemical shifts of excited states at 0.5–5% occupancy. This is arguably the highest-information ensemble data that exists.
  • Residual dipolar couplings and PREs. Long-range orientational and distance restraints that are ensemble averages, so they constrain the distribution rather than a single structure. Central to intrinsically disordered protein work.
  • SAXS/SANS. Low resolution but genuinely ensemble-averaged, cheap, and sensitive to global shape distributions. Time-resolved variants capture transitions.
  • Cryo-EM heterogeneity. The unmined resource. Every dataset contains a distribution of states, and continuous heterogeneity methods now extract landscapes rather than discrete classes. Raw particle stacks in EMPIAR are, in effect, unlabeled ensemble data at large scale.
  • Room-temperature and time-resolved crystallography. Alternate conformers, occupancies, B-factor structure; XFEL work gives structural intermediates on femtosecond-to-millisecond timescales.

Thermodynamic and energetic anchors

Absolute numbers with real units, which is what keeps the model from drifting into arbitrary scales.

  • ITC. ΔG, ΔH, ΔS, and stoichiometry separately, which is rare and valuable — most methods give you only the free energy.
  • DSC and thermal/chemical denaturation. ΔCp, m-values, cooperativity, and unfolding intermediates.
  • ProTherm-type ΔΔG compilations. Tens of thousands, heterogeneous quality.
  • Redox potentiometry and pKa measurements. Small in number but directly the control variables for the photosynthesis-class problems.
  • Volume and pressure data. ΔV of folding, activation volumes. Small, distinctive, and constrains packing physics in a way nothing else does.

Single-molecule and force

Small datasets, unique content: these are the only measurements that give you full distributions and force-dependence.

  • Optical tweezers and AFM force spectroscopy. Force-dependent unfolding rates, energy landscape parameters (Δx‡), catch-bond behavior. Directly the ∂rate/∂force susceptibility.
  • smFRET. Distance distributions and interconversion kinetics on individual molecules — populations and rates without ensemble averaging.
  • Single-molecule motor assays. Step size, stall force, velocity versus load and versus ATP concentration. This is the nonequilibrium machine data, and there isn't much of it.

Spectroscopic and electronic

Where your photosynthesis ambitions get grounded.

  • Vibrational Stark spectroscopy. Local electric fields in active sites, in volts per angstrom. Few systems, but it's a direct measurement of a quantity almost nothing predicts.
  • Transient absorption and 2D electronic spectroscopy. Energy transfer pathways and timescales; site energies and couplings.
  • EPR and DEER. Spin state, metal coordination, and distance distributions between labels — distributions, again, not points.
  • Infrared and Raman. Bond-level readout of protonation, hydrogen bonding, and metal-ligand structure.

Nonequilibrium and cellular

The scarcest and, for machines, the most on-target.

  • Enzyme kinetics beyond kcat/KM. Pre-steady-state, isotope effects (which report on transition-state structure), pH-rate profiles, viscosity dependence.
  • Motor and pump thermodynamics. Efficiency, coupling ratios, reversal points, load dependence.
  • Bioelectrical measurements. Patch clamp gives you single-channel conductance and voltage-dependent gating with real statistics, and the datasets are large. Voltage as an explicit input variable, which almost no model has.
  • In-cell NMR and quinary interaction measurements. Behavior in the actual crowded environment.

Three things worth flagging about how to use this

Negative and failure data is missing and matters. Every failed design, every dead mutant, every protein that wouldn't express. This is the most systematically discarded information in the field, and for a world model it's as informative as success. Building the infrastructure to capture it might be a contribution in itself.

Prioritize measurements that are already derivatives. Differential HDX, ΔΔG, drug-shifted melting curves, force-dependent rates, pH-rate profiles. These match the susceptibility framing from earlier, they cancel systematic error, and they're what design specifications are written in.

The unifying trick is a differentiable forward model per observable. If you can compute the predicted HDX protection, RDC, SAXS curve, chemical shift, or FRET efficiency from an ensemble, then every one of these heterogeneous measurements becomes a loss on the same underlying object. That's how you train one model on all of it without inventing a separate head per assay — and forward models exist, in usable form, for most of what's listed above.

That last point may be the practical core of the whole data strategy. The ensemble is the latent variable; the experiments are noisy projections of it; the model learns to produce ensembles whose projections match everything at once.

Textbook mechanism is real knowledge, but it's a specific kind of knowledge, and the mismatch with what you need is sharper than it first appears.

What the corpus actually contains

Take the deepest cases. Serine protease catalysis: you get the charge-relay geometry, the tetrahedral intermediate, the oxyanion hole, the acylation-deacylation sequence, the rate enhancement over background of about 10¹⁰. Myosin: nucleotide states mapped to lever-arm positions, the order of product release, which step is rate-limiting, the ~36 nm step. Hemoglobin: T and R states, the Perutz mechanism, Bohr effect, 2,3-BPG. Photosystem II: the S-state cycle, the Mn cluster geometry, the Yz tyrosine relay.

Notice what these have in common. They're topologies with sparse quantitative annotation. A state graph, an ordering of events, a claim about which interactions matter, and a handful of numbers — usually a kcat, sometimes a Kd, occasionally a barrier height. What they don't contain is a free energy surface, coordinates for intermediates, the ensemble at any state, or the values of the interactions being invoked.

The depth is also wildly non-uniform. Perhaps a few hundred systems are known at that level. Thousands more have a proposed mechanism with major gaps. The overwhelming majority of proteins have an assigned family and a guess. And the deeply-studied set is biased toward small, soluble, stable, crystallizable, commercially available enzymes — precisely not the membrane machines and metalloenzymes you'd need for the photosynthesis-class problems.

The reliability problem is worse than people assume

Textbook mechanisms are consensus interpretations, and they're revised. The proton wire in bacteriorhodopsin has been argued about for decades. Enzyme dynamics contributing to catalysis was mainstream, then heavily contested, and remains unsettled. Kinetic isotope effects and hydrogen tunneling in ADH have been reinterpreted more than once. RuBisCO's mechanism was refined repeatedly. The Mn₄CaO₅ O–O bond formation step has multiple live competing proposals right now.

If you train a model on textbook consensus, you're training it on a particular decade's interpretation and encoding contested claims as ground truth. That's a different failure than noisy data — it's confidently wrong in exactly the places where the field's understanding is most likely to shift, and a model that reproduces textbook mechanism perfectly has learned to reproduce human interpretation, not physics.

There's a deeper issue too. Mechanistic language is heavily anthropomorphic and conceptually loaded. "Induced fit," "proton wire," "hydrophobic collapse," "conformational gating" are compressed descriptions that fit human causal intuition. They are not guaranteed to carve the physics at its joints. Encoding them is exactly the mechanistic prior you said you wanted to avoid.

Where it genuinely helps

Despite all that, three uses seem sound.

Constraints rather than labels. This is the strongest form. Textbook knowledge says: this residue contacts substrate in the transition state; these two states are connected and these are not; this state precedes that one; this mutation abolishes activity. Those are cheap, robust, and mostly survive mechanistic revision — the topology is more stable than the interpretation. Feed them as constraints on generated ensembles rather than as targets to predict. That's compatible with the constraint-based supervision idea from earlier and doesn't bake anything into the architecture.

Ensemble specification. Your simulator needs to know what to sample. Textbook knowledge tells you a kinesin cycle has these states in this order with these nucleotides bound — that's a scaffold for generating trajectories to then be selected among by experimental data. Using it to define the space rather than to label points keeps it in the featurizer role.

Negative and impossibility knowledge. The corpus records a great deal about what doesn't work: mutations that kill activity, reactions that don't proceed, chemistries biology never evolved. Underused, and it constrains the model from a direction data alone doesn't.

Where it doesn't help

Anything requiring numbers. Textbooks give you the shape of a free energy landscape verbally — "a high barrier," "a stabilized intermediate" — and you cannot recover kT-scale quantities from adjectives. If your target output is rates and susceptibilities, the corpus is qualitatively rich and quantitatively empty, which is the wrong way around.

Also: mechanisms are usually described for one system at a time, with no consistent formalism. Extracting them into a machine-usable form is a real undertaking. Reaction databases like Rhea, MACiE, and M-CSA have done this for enzyme chemistry, and they're worth building on, but they cover chemistry rather than the conformational and energetic mechanism you care about.

A more interesting framing

The corpus is best understood as a set of solved instances of the inverse problem you're trying to solve. Every well-characterized mechanism is an existence proof: this arrangement of atoms achieves this function through this pathway. You want the map from function to structure, and the corpus gives you a few hundred verified points on that map, with reasoning attached.

That's small for training and potentially valuable for evaluation. A sharper test of your world model than any benchmark: given the system and conditions but not the mechanism, does the model's generated ensemble reproduce the known mechanism? Does it identify the catalytic residues? Does it recover the state ordering? Held-out mechanisms are the ideal eval set precisely because they're few, deeply verified, and require the model to have gotten the causal structure right rather than to have interpolated.

I'd use the corpus that way first. Evaluation before training. If it turns out the model recovers known mechanisms it was never shown, you've learned something about whether the physics-first thesis works — and that's worth more than the marginal training signal you'd have gotten from feeding it in.

Toward a Physical World Model for Molecular Systems

A working thesis, its open forks, and a data strategy

1. The core claim, restated

The original framing was that evolutionary couplings are insufficient because they lack physics. That version of the argument is weaker than it needs to be, and it invites the obvious rebuttal that force fields have had physics for forty years without solving design.

The sharper claim is about support. Coevolutionary models can only place probability mass where evolution has already been. Physics matters instrumentally, because the set of physically realizable systems is vastly larger than the set of evolutionarily occupied ones and is better defined. The contribution is therefore a generative prior over physically realizable molecular systems, not a model that "understands" physics in some general sense.

This also clarifies what the work is not competing with. Force fields and quantum chemistry are transferable and roughly correct; they fail as proposal distributions, not as scorers. The gap being filled is generative and inverse.

One caveat should be held consciously rather than argued away. Natural sequences encode solutions to constraints that are not yet formalized: folding kinetics, aggregation avoidance, expressibility, proteostatic compatibility. Physics will not supply these cheaply. A defensible synthesis retains evolutionary information as a prior over manufacturability while physics supplies the generative support.

2. The output object

This is the single largest unresolved fork, and most downstream choices follow from it. Candidates: coordinates, ensembles, free energy surfaces, response functions, or driven cycles.

The argument developed here favors susceptibilities — derivatives of observables with respect to control variables:

∂(conformation)∂(force),∂(activity)∂(pH),∂(rate)∂(Δμ)\frac{\partial(\text{conformation})}{\partial(\text{force})}, \quad \frac{\partial(\text{activity})}{\partial(\text{pH})}, \quad \frac{\partial(\text{rate})}{\partial(\Delta\mu)}∂(force)∂(conformation)​,∂(pH)∂(activity)​,∂(Δμ)∂(rate)​

Four reasons this is the right native output:

  1. Response functions are what an engineer specifies. Design intent is written in changes, not absolutes.
  2. They are directly measurable, and many existing datasets are already differential in form.
  3. At equilibrium they are linked to fluctuations by fluctuation–dissipation, giving a theoretical handle.
  4. Their breakdown far from equilibrium is precisely the regime where molecular machines operate.

A model whose native output is a response tensor rather than coordinates would be a categorically different object from anything currently in the field.

Two adjacent commitments follow:

  • Free energy, not potential energy. Nearly all ML potentials learn potential energy and recover thermodynamics by sampling. A model that learns transferable free energies at some coarse resolution — solvent and entropy folded in — sidesteps the sampling bottleneck. The theory of learned, controlled-error coarse-grainings is underdeveloped and is itself a plausible foundational contribution.
  • Driven cycles as the target for machines. How chemical energy input couples to directed conformational change, with rates. Almost nothing in ML targets this, and it is where a genuinely new subfield could sit. Allostery and mechanochemical coupling are the same object viewed from different angles.

The extrapolation claim needs a mechanism

Physics generalizes because of locality, symmetry, and compositionality. The thesis should state explicitly which of these are hard-coded and which are learned. An extrapolation claim without a structural mechanism behind it is a hope, and reviewers will find it.

A missing piece: the specification language

Design is an inverse problem, and at present the only thing specifiable with any precision is a shape. "Respond this way to this perturbation," "have this landscape topology," "have this ratio of rates" are not expressible in any current formalism. A formal language for molecular function, differentiable with respect to design variables, may be as significant a contribution as the model itself.

3. The environment: what current models cannot represent

Light, pH, and temperature are the obvious omissions. The full space is much larger, and it divides usefully into two categories that require different machinery.

Thermodynamic conditions (a conditioning problem)

ClassVariables
MechanicalForce and tension (load-dependent kinetics, catch bonds); pressure and ΔV; shear and flow; torsion and supercoiling
ElectricalTransmembrane potential (~10⁷ V/m); local active-site fields; interfacial potentials and dielectric heterogeneity
Chemical potentialATP/ADP·Pi ratio rather than [ATP]; redox potential and glutathione ratio; proton motive force; water activity
SolventIonic strength and specific ion effects; osmolytes and preferential exclusion; crowding at 300–400 g/L; viscosity and friction
InterfacesMembrane lateral pressure, curvature, hydrophobic mismatch; condensate interiors; confinement in cages, pores, exit tunnels
Covalent statePTMs as environment-coupled control variables; metallation and mis-metallation; cofactor loading

The distinction between chemical potential and concentration deserves emphasis. Distance from equilibrium determines what a machine can do; at equilibrium a motor is merely a shuttle.

Process and history (a trajectory problem)

These cannot be handled by adding an input channel, because they break the ensemble picture entirely:

  • Cotranslational folding. Structure depends on vectorial emergence and codon-level pausing. The native state is sometimes not the global minimum.
  • Kinetic trapping and hysteresis. Serpins and amyloid are functional or pathological because they are trapped.
  • Chaperone-assisted paths, proline isomerization, slow maturation.
  • Driving frequency. Environments that oscillate faster or slower than relaxation produce different behavior from identical systems. Ratcheting and resonance live here.

System-level effects

Effective molarity from tethering and multivalency, spanning orders of magnitude. Quinary interactions — nonspecific stickiness to everything else in the cell. Feedback, where a system alters the environment it responds to: a pump changes the pH that gates it.

4. Photosynthesis as the stress test

Photosynthesis is the right benchmark because the entire chain from photon to stored bond is a sequence of rate ratios rather than shapes. The specification for a reaction center is literally a set of potentials, rates, and yields. A framework that cannot express that cannot express this problem.

Decomposition

  1. Light harvesting. Position ~100 chromophores so excitation funnels to one site at near-unit efficiency in picoseconds. The design variable is the exciton Hamiltonian: site energies tuned by protein electrostatics over hundreds of cm⁻¹, couplings set by geometry. Most tractable piece; maquettes already work.
  2. Charge separation. The essential trick is kinetic asymmetry — forward transfer beating recombination by orders of magnitude, with recombination pushed into the Marcus inverted region. PSII is structurally pseudo-symmetric but only one branch is active, and nobody can design that from first principles.
  3. Water oxidation. The wall. Four electrons, four protons, O–O bond formation across five S-states over milliseconds, holding a hole at ~1.25 V — above the oxidation potential of most nearby amino acids. Mechanism still contested.
  4. Vectorial organization. Charge must separate across a membrane; protons released one side, consumed the other. Topology is part of the design object, not context.
  5. Photoprotection and repair. PSII destroys itself; D1 is replaced on a ~30-minute cycle under illumination. Any system at these potentials generates singlet oxygen. The design must either survive its own chemistry or be sacrificial by construction.
  6. Downstream fixation. Probably drop it. RuBisCO is slow and promiscuous.

Capability gaps this exposes

  • Excited states. Everything upstream of charge separation is electronic structure in the excited manifold within a fluctuating protein field. Ground-state ML potentials are the wrong object. This is where the "electron boundary" decision stops being optional.
  • Redox potentials to sub-100 mV in protein, as a function of protonation state.
  • Rates spanning eighteen orders of magnitude, where femtosecond processes determine whether hour-scale ones occur.
  • Transition metal clusters with strong correlation, multiple oxidation states, spin coupling. DFT struggles; training data at scale does not exist.

The reframe that makes it tractable

Biology's photosynthesis is contingent, not optimal. Water is a poor electron donor chosen for abundance; chlorophyll wastes the green; solar-to-biomass efficiency in crops is around one percent.

So the target should be specification, not recapitulation:

Design a light-driven system that separates charge at high quantum yield and drives a specified uphill redox reaction against a defined chemical potential gradient, with a defined operational lifetime.

The donor need not be water, the chromophore need not be chlorophyll, the catalyst need not be manganese. This is a genuine test of the framework rather than a reverse-engineering exercise, and it stages cleanly:

energy transfer between synthetic chromophores with controlled site energies → charge separation with a designed forward/recombination ratio → vectorial across a bilayer → driving an actual uphill reaction

Each stage is publishable, each sits at or just past the frontier, each exercises the claimed capability. Water oxidation remains the terminal grand challenge and may require electronic-structure advances orthogonal to this program.

5. Synthetic data: what it can and cannot buy

Difficulty varies by roughly six orders of magnitude across the relevant physics, and the hard cases are not fixed by compute.

RegimeStatusNote
Ground-state organic energies/forcesSolvedBottleneck is configurational coverage, not labels
Excited states, single chromophoresExpensive but honestTD-DFT fails on charge-transfer states; multireference methods need human judgment on active space, blocking automation
Transition metal clustersGenerator is wrong where the model needs to be rightFunctionals disagree on spin-state ordering and mechanism. Training here yields a fast DFT emulator that reproduces DFT's specific errors
Rates and free energiesNot a data-generation problemCPU-months per label, and the collective variable is often the unsolved problem itself. Hundreds of labels, not millions

The circularity trap, stated sharply

Synthetic data transfers the generator's beliefs, not nature's. Training on MD yields a model that predicts what the force field would have said. Useful as an accelerator; useless as a source of new physical insight.

Note the uncomfortable implication: the observables identified in §2 as most distinctive — response functions, rate ratios — sit precisely in the regime where labels are scarcest.

Practical mitigations

  • Stratify deliberately. Millions of cheap labels for representation and locality; thousands of expensive ones for correction; hundreds of experimental anchors against drift.
  • Δ-learning. Predict the difference between cheap and expensive methods. The difference is smoother and lower-variance than either. The most reliable trick available.
  • Adaptive spending. Uncertainty-driven active learning routinely buys 10–100× reduction in high-level calculations. At CASPT2 scale, that is the difference between infeasible and merely painful.
  • Labels with error bars, trained as such. If each label carries a variance estimate, the model learns to distrust regions where the generator was shaky. Almost nobody does this, and given a dataset heterogeneous in reliability it may be the highest-leverage methodological choice available.

The genuinely novel alternative: constraint-based supervision

Train on physical constraints rather than examples: detailed balance, thermodynamic cycle closure, Maxwell relations between response functions, symmetry and gauge conditions, known asymptotic limits. These require no calculation and constrain the model in ways that generalize outside any sampled distribution.

Since synthetic data cannot by construction take a model outside its generator's distribution, and since the thesis is fundamentally about extrapolation, this may be the only route that delivers what is being claimed. It warrants treatment as a central pillar rather than a regularization detail.

6. The simulator as stochastic featurizer

There is an existing name for the approach of grounding simulations in real measurements: amortized simulation-based inference. Its failure modes are documented rather than waiting to surprise you.

The reframe

Stop treating the simulator as a data generator that must be accurate. Treat it as a map from (sequence, conditions) to a distribution over plausible dynamical realizations, with the experimental measurement as the label.

This replaces the requirement of accuracy with the requirement of coverage. The true behavior need only lie within the support of what the simulator produces; the learning problem becomes selection rather than correction. Running a deliberately bad-but-diverse simulator — poor force field, aggressive enhanced sampling, wide parameter randomization — becomes principled rather than a compromise. This is domain randomization, transported from robotics.

Corollary: coverage is now the engineering target and should be measured directly. A simulator that is more accurate but narrower is worse for this purpose.

Why the causal claim is stronger than it first appears

Simulation provides interventional, not observational, data. Mutate, change pH, apply force, vary one thing under randomized everything-else. No confounders, because the generating process is under control. Most causal inference fights to approximate what a simulator supplies for free.

The bet being made is that the simulator gets the causal topology right while getting magnitudes wrong. For sterics, electrostatics, packing, and hydrogen bonding this is defensible — MD gets the qualitative consequence of a mutation right far more often than it gets ΔΔG right. For bond formation, metal chemistry, and excited states the topology itself is wrong and the bet fails. Each experiment should be explicitly assigned to one regime or the other.

The failure mode that will actually kill it

Shortcut learning between positive and negative generation procedures. If positives and negatives differ in any pipeline detail — sampling length, setup, provenance — the model finds the artifact immediately, reports high accuracy, and learns nothing. This is the most common way contrastive setups on simulated data fail quietly.

Mitigations are strict:

  • Positives and negatives from an identical procedure differing only in the causal variable.
  • Validation on held-out systems whose ensembles were generated by a different simulator entirely. If performance survives a force field swap, physics was learned. If not, the pipeline was learned.

Train on contrasts

Systematic simulator bias largely cancels in differences. Predicting Δ(observable) between mutant and wild-type through the same broken simulator is far more robust than predicting absolutes. It also matches what design requires and what large datasets already contain.

The honest bitter-lesson objection

This architecture is a prior. It asserts that dynamical ensembles are the right intermediate representation. The maximally pure position would skip the simulator entirely: sequence + conditions → observable, scaled up.

Two defenses, both worth holding consciously:

  1. It is a prior about representation, not mechanism. Not "here is how allostery works," but "trajectories are a useful input format" — the same category as pixels for vision.
  2. The bitter lesson concerns the asymptotic regime, and this project is not in it. With a few thousand measured rates, the direct approach has no chance; the simulator buys sample efficiency. The prior is a bridge, and should be expected to burn later.

Where the ceiling actually sits

Not compute — simulation is cheap and embarrassingly parallel. The binding constraint is the number of real measurements available for anchoring. This makes experimental data partnerships potentially more strategically important than GPUs.

7. The measurement landscape

Usefulness is governed by two quantities that trade off almost perfectly: how many measurements exist, and how much dynamical information each carries. Hence stratification.

High-throughput, low information per point

Representation-learning substrate. Individually thin, collectively enormous, inherently comparative.

  • Deep mutational scans — 10⁴–10⁶ variants per experiment; readouts span stability (mega-scale proteolysis datasets), binding, activity, expression, localization. Some run at multiple temperatures or ligand concentrations, making them response measurements.
  • Thermal proteome profiling — melting shifts across thousands of proteins in cells; effectively ∂stability/∂(ligand) at proteome scale.
  • Limited proteolysis MS — proteome-wide conformational change.
  • Chemical genetics / stress panels — pH, osmolyte, temperature, oxidant. Noisy, abundant, directly encodes environmental coupling.
  • Directed evolution trajectories — underused; each campaign is a partially ordered record of causal interventions.

Mid-throughput, ensemble-resolved

The sweet spot: each measurement constrains a distribution rather than a point.

  • HDX-MS — per-peptide protection factors are local free energies of opening. Differential HDX most useful.
  • NMR relaxation — order parameters for ps–ns amplitudes; relaxation dispersion gives populations, rates, and shifts of excited states at 0.5–5% occupancy. Arguably the highest-information ensemble data in existence.
  • RDCs and PREs — long-range orientational and distance restraints, ensemble-averaged by nature.
  • SAXS/SANS — low resolution, genuinely ensemble-averaged, cheap; time-resolved variants capture transitions.
  • Cryo-EM heterogeneity — the unmined resource. Continuous heterogeneity methods extract landscapes rather than discrete classes; raw particle stacks are unlabeled ensemble data at scale.
  • Room-temperature and time-resolved crystallography — alternate conformers, occupancies; XFEL gives femtosecond-to-millisecond intermediates.

Thermodynamic anchors

Absolute numbers in real units; these prevent scale drift.

ITC (ΔG, ΔH, ΔS separately — rare and valuable); DSC and denaturation (ΔCp, m-values, cooperativity); ProTherm-type ΔΔG compilations; redox potentiometry and pKa measurements (directly the control variables for §4); volume and pressure data (small, distinctive, uniquely constrains packing).

Single-molecule and force

Small datasets, unique content — the only source of full distributions and force dependence.

Optical tweezers and AFM (force-dependent rates, Δx‡, catch bonds — directly ∂rate/∂force); smFRET (distance distributions and interconversion kinetics without ensemble averaging); motor assays (step size, stall force, velocity vs. load and [ATP] — the scarce nonequilibrium machine data).

Spectroscopic and electronic

Where the photosynthesis ambition gets grounded.

Vibrational Stark spectroscopy (local fields in V/Å — a direct measurement of something almost nothing predicts); transient absorption and 2D electronic spectroscopy (transfer pathways, site energies, couplings); EPR and DEER (spin state, coordination, distance distributions); IR and Raman (protonation, H-bonding, metal-ligand structure).

Nonequilibrium and cellular

Scarcest, and for machines the most on-target.

Enzyme kinetics beyond kcat/KM — pre-steady-state, isotope effects (transition-state structure), pH-rate profiles, viscosity dependence. Motor and pump thermodynamics — efficiency, coupling ratios, reversal points. Patch clamp — single-channel conductance and voltage-dependent gating, with large datasets and voltage as an explicit input variable that no current model has. In-cell NMR and quinary interaction measurements.

Three cross-cutting points

Negative and failure data is missing and matters. Every failed design, dead mutant, and protein that would not express. The most systematically discarded information in the field, and for a world model as informative as success. Building infrastructure to capture it could be a contribution in itself.

Prioritize measurements that are already derivatives. Differential HDX, ΔΔG, drug-shifted melting curves, force-dependent rates, pH-rate profiles. These match the susceptibility framing, cancel systematic error, and are written in the language of design specifications.

The unifying mechanism is a differentiable forward model per observable. If predicted HDX protection, RDCs, SAXS curves, chemical shifts, and FRET efficiencies can all be computed from an ensemble, every heterogeneous measurement becomes a loss on the same underlying object — no separate head per assay. Forward models exist in usable form for most of the above.

This is the practical core of the data strategy: the ensemble is the latent variable, experiments are noisy projections of it, and the model learns to produce ensembles whose projections match everything at once.

8. The descriptive biochemistry corpus

What it actually contains

Textbook mechanisms are topologies with sparse quantitative annotation: a state graph, an ordering of events, a claim about which interactions matter, and a handful of numbers. Serine proteases give charge-relay geometry, the tetrahedral intermediate, the oxyanion hole, ~10¹⁰ rate enhancement. Myosin gives nucleotide states mapped to lever-arm positions, product release order, the rate-limiting step. What is absent: free energy surfaces, intermediate coordinates, ensembles at any state, values for the interactions being invoked.

Depth is wildly non-uniform. Perhaps a few hundred systems are known at that level; thousands have proposed mechanisms with major gaps; the majority have a family assignment and a guess. The well-studied set is biased toward small, soluble, stable, crystallizable, commercially available enzymes — precisely not the membrane machines and metalloenzymes needed for §4.

Two reliability problems

Mechanisms get revised. Bacteriorhodopsin's proton wire, the role of dynamics in catalysis, hydrogen tunneling in ADH, the O–O bond formation step in PSII — all contested or repeatedly reinterpreted. Training on textbook consensus encodes one decade's interpretation as ground truth, and does so confidently in exactly the places most likely to shift.

Mechanistic language carries unwanted priors. "Induced fit," "proton wire," "hydrophobic collapse," "conformational gating" are compressed descriptions fitted to human causal intuition. They are not guaranteed to carve the physics at its joints. Encoding them is precisely the mechanistic prior the architecture is meant to avoid.

Three sound uses

  1. Constraints, not labels. This residue contacts substrate in the transition state; these states are connected and these are not; this ordering holds; this mutation abolishes activity. Cheap, robust, and largely surviving mechanistic revision — topology is more stable than interpretation. Apply as constraints on generated ensembles, not as prediction targets. Compatible with §5's constraint-based supervision.
  2. Ensemble specification. The simulator needs to know what to sample. "A kinesin cycle has these states in this order with these nucleotides bound" is a scaffold for trajectory generation, with experimental data doing the selection. Keeps the corpus in the featurizer role.
  3. Impossibility knowledge. What does not work: mutations that kill activity, reactions that do not proceed, chemistries evolution never found. Constrains the model from a direction data alone does not.

Reaction databases (Rhea, MACiE, M-CSA) have machine-usable enzyme chemistry and are worth building on, though they cover chemistry rather than conformational and energetic mechanism.

The strongest use is evaluation, not training

The corpus is best understood as a set of solved instances of the inverse problem. Every characterized mechanism is an existence proof: this arrangement of atoms achieves this function via this pathway. The map from function to structure is exactly what the model is meant to learn, and the corpus provides a few hundred verified points on it, with reasoning attached.

That is small for training and potentially decisive for evaluation:

Given the system and conditions but not the mechanism, does the model's generated ensemble reproduce the known mechanism? Does it identify the catalytic residues? Does it recover the state ordering?

Held-out mechanisms are an ideal eval set precisely because they are few, deeply verified, and require the causal structure to be right rather than interpolated. Recovering mechanisms the model was never shown would be direct evidence for the physics-first thesis — worth more than the marginal training signal from feeding them in.

Recommendation: evaluation before training.

9. Open decisions

These determine everything downstream and are not yet settled:

  1. What is the output object? Coordinates, ensembles, free energy surfaces, response tensors, or driven cycles. The argument here favors response functions with cycles as the machine-specific target.
  2. Where is the electron boundary? Inside the model, an oracle, or out of scope. Determines whether photosynthesis-class problems are reachable at all.
  3. What is the canonical hard problem? A subfield needs a shared object, a benchmark, and a case where existing methods provably cannot compete. Candidates: non-natural backbones and foldamers; de novo catalysis with transition-state stabilization; programmable ligand-gated switches with target ΔΔG; a synthetic motor with defined directionality; the staged photosynthesis program of §4.
  4. How central is constraint-based supervision? Regularization detail or central pillar. The extrapolation claim may depend on the answer.
  5. What is retained from evolution? Full rejection, or retention as a manufacturability prior.
  6. Where does the experimental anchoring come from? Given that measurement count, not compute, is the binding constraint, which partnerships or data-generation efforts are on the critical path.

10. Summary of the strongest positions

  • Physics matters because it widens the support, not because force fields are inaccurate.
  • The native output should be susceptibilities — response functions are what design specifies, what experiments measure, and what breaks informatively out of equilibrium.
  • The environment splits into conditions (a conditioning problem) and processes (a trajectory problem); the second cannot be handled by adding input channels.
  • Photosynthesis is the right stress test, but only in its specification form, staged into four frontier-adjacent milestones.
  • Synthetic data transfers the generator's beliefs; Δ-learning, active learning, per-label error bars, and physical constraints are the mitigations.
  • The simulator is a stochastic featurizer where coverage beats accuracy, supplying interventional data whose causal topology is trustworthy in some regimes and not others.
  • The measurement landscape should be exploited through differentiable forward models onto a shared latent ensemble, prioritizing observables that are already derivatives.
  • The descriptive corpus is a benchmark, not a training set — and possibly the sharpest available test of whether the whole thesis holds.
